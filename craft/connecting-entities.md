# Connecting Entities

A world written in clusters produces dense islands joined only through its most famous entities. Degree and reachability report it as well connected, because everything reaches everything through a god. `make web WORLD=<id>` asks the question those measures cannot: what still holds together once the top prominence tiers are removed.

An entity left alone under that cut touches nothing at its own scale. A component that separates is an island whose only route out runs through something renowned.

This procedure fills those gaps with **new entities**. Adding an edge between two existing things records a connection somebody already imagined; writing what sits between them adds world.

## 1. Measure

Run `make web WORLD=<id>`. Two cuts are reported: the graph without its mythic entities, and without mythic and renowned together. Read the second.

The entities listed as alone are the work list. So are the islands, which need joining to each other rather than to the centre.

## 2. Choose a pair

Take two entities from different components, both recognized or lower, and prefer pairs in different regions, civilizations or kinds.

Skip any pair whose prose already claims a relationship. That is a missing edge, not a missing entity — declare the edge and choose again.

## 3. Name three candidates before choosing one

Ask what a person would actually encounter moving between the two. Useful shapes:

- an intermediary who serves neither party
- an object both sides must use, and neither owns
- a place where the two systems physically meet
- a trade that exists because the gap exists
- a route, and what it costs to travel
- a case, claim or dispute both were parties to
- a recurring occasion both attend

Write down three before committing to one. The first candidate is usually the most available one rather than the best.

## 4. Keep the candidate that is new

The connector is a new entity. If an existing one is the obvious answer, that pair was already connected and the real gap is elsewhere — return to step 2.

Prefer the candidate that would exist in dozens of copies across the world, and write the single instance that matters here. A thing that exists once and happens to join two distant places is the same small world arriving in another shape.

## 5. Prefer objects, places, procedures and cases; let people be rare

A named intermediary is the most natural bridge and the fastest route back to a small world, where every connection eventually runs through the same few fixers.

Write a person when the connection is genuinely personal — an obligation, a debt, a shared history, a professional judgement somebody is known for. Do not write the same person into a second bridge.

## 6. Accept a chain

Two things that genuinely connect through two intermediate parties get the middle one written now. The far end waits for a later pass.

A single connector spanning half the world is the availability bias again.

## 7. Set prominence below both endpoints

A connector between two recognized entities is marginal. Below that, forgotten.

This keeps connectors from accumulating into a second tier of famous things, and the prominence-reach check then holds them to being named only from entities adjacent to them.

## 8. Route the edges laterally

The new entity's relationships go to its two endpoints. If the draft needs a mythic or renowned entity to make sense, the connector is wrong — return to step 3.

## 9. Write it

Follow the world's own authoring procedure: choose the observer whose knowledge bounds the entry, pull an outside source of detail, and add every relationship the prose establishes.

## 10. Stop

Re-run `make web`. The pass is finished for that pair when the two components are joined with no mythic or renowned entity on the path between them.
