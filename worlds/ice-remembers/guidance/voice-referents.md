# Voice referents

## Valid in-world terms

Penguin, orca, chick, pod, rookery, berg, shelf, floe, aurora, Long Dark,
Endless Day, Thaw, Freeze, ice-memory, shadow, fire-core, crystal, pressure,
magic, guild, council, law, market, archive, and chronicle are ordinary terms
on Aurora Berg.

Use a named local place or institution when the comparison depends on culture.
Universal bodily and physical comparisons are safe: cold skin, cracking ice,
deep pressure, reflected light, hunger, exhaustion, and the weight of another
body in a huddle.

## Keep out of lore prose

- Earth, human history, real countries, real cities, and real religions
- Arctic and Antarctic as external geographic labels; say the high ice, open
  water, bottom of the world, or name the place
- cars, phones, television, modern offices, and other comparisons that assume a
  different society
- game, player, campaign, simulation, seed, generation, model, prompt, and
  content
- wiki, entry, database, schema, tag, entity ID, and anything described as not
  yet written

The source chronology uses ticks. A fact card or timeline may display a tick,
but a chronicler uses the named era, season, or recorded event unless a source
document explicitly counts ticks.

## Attribution

Replace anonymous uncertainty with its holder and evidence. Write that a
Wake-Singer teaching-song names the Corpse Current as divine blood, that an
Aurora reader found a pressure mark in a given ice layer, or that Nightshelf
ledgers omit a shipment. Do not write that outsiders cannot say, that something
may be more than it seems, or that the truth waits to be discovered.

Imported canonical text is preserved before cleanup. Apply these substitutions
when writing new prose or making an explicit, logged correction; do not silently
modernize a Chronicle during migration.
