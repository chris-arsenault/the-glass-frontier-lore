# Naming conventions

## Existing names

Preserve every published name exactly during migration, including case,
spacing, apostrophes, tildes, crescents, therefore marks, parentheses, and
hyphenated epithets. Keep the bundle slug in `source_id`; use the repository's
underscored symbol and hyphenated filename conventions for Lorecraft IDs.

Do not repair an unusual name by overwriting it. Add a public alias when readers
need another form. Rename only through an explicit editorial change with a log.

## Aurora names

Aurora names accumulate public evidence: offices, mentors, places, witnessed
deeds, and earned epithets can extend a name. Parentheses and possessive clan
forms belong to the recorded name, not to narrator commentary. A long name must
point to specific history rather than merely sound ceremonial.

## Nightshelf names

Nightshelf names withhold part of themselves. The marks `☽`, `~`, and `∴`
indicate hidden, faded, or swallowed portions in the published naming system.
Use them deliberately and keep the visible fragment pronounceable. Do not make
every Nightshelf term an opaque string; occupations, routes, tools, and public
places still need names other people can use.

## Orca names

Orca names carry deeds, lineage, scars, kills, songs, and remembered changes in
status. Hard consonants and repeated sounds occur in the record, but an orca
name must remain distinct from nearby names and usable in prose. A deed-name
records something the orca or pod recognizes; it is not a narrator's insult.

## New names

Check `search` before adding one. Prefer a name tied to a visible feature,
office, route, material, recorded deed, or local practice. Avoid near-duplicates
that differ only by punctuation and names whose only purpose is to sound alien.
